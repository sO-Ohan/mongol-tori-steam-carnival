01 / Who we are

Nobody handed
us a robot

In 2015 a few students at BRAC University decided to build a Mars rover. They had no rover, no lab of their own, and no idea how.

Eleven years later there are six teams inside our team — metal, electronics, code, radios, biology, and the part that makes it drive itself. Every single one of us is a student.

You do not start with the skill. You start with the curiosity.

Our team, Utah, May 2026

Mars Desert Research Station · USA

This is not Mars

It is a desert in Utah, USA — the closest thing to Mars we have on Earth. Every summer, rover teams from all over the world drive here to find out whose machine actually works.

116 teams · 18 countries 38 made it to the desert We finished 7th

02 / Why robots go first

Mars does not want you there

−60°C Average temperature on Mars. Colder than any freezer you have opened.
0% Oxygen you could breathe. The air is mostly carbon dioxide.
20min A radio message can take this long to reach Mars. You cannot say "stop!" in time.
0 Mechanics on Mars. If it breaks there, it stays broken.

So Mars gets the robots first. Nobody can practise at −60 °C, so we practise in that Utah desert instead — about +35 °C in June — where the dust, the rocks and the distance are real. You can start the same way here.

03 / Meet the machine

Taurus

Ears

Three radios on one mast. It listens to us from over a kilometre away.

Arm

Seven joints and a gripper. It picks up tools, turns switches, plugs in cables.

Brain

A computer inside the body, plus circuit boards we designed ourselves.

Wheels

No air inside them — nothing to puncture. The frame bends so all four keep gripping.

04 / What it has to do

Four jobs. Thirty minutes each.

01Dig

Scoop up soil and test it, right there, for any sign that something once lived.

02Deliver

Find a toolbox, pick it up, carry it across rocks to an astronaut who needs it.

03Repair

Flip switches, turn valves and plug cables into a broken lander — with a robot hand.

04Think

Drive to a place on a map and back. Nobody steering. Nobody helping.

05 / The hardest rule

The driver is not allowed to see the rover

Our driver sits in a tent, a kilometre away, with the rover completely out of sight. Everything they know comes through a camera and a radio — this much of the world, and nothing else.

06 / How it hears us

Invisible waves,
through a hill

The same thing that carries radio songs to your house carries our commands to the rover. We run three different radios at the same time.

Why three? Because one of them will fail on the day. When it does, the other two are already carrying the rover home.

1.2km The distance we drove it — with hills in the way
3 Radios live at once, so losing one is survivable
Two of them. One stays with us, one rides the rover.

Hold your hand up now — you are standing in radio waves. You just cannot feel them.

07 / Driving itself

Close your eyes and walk to the door

That is the rover's job — every day, on ground it has never seen. It has four senses, and every one of them lies a little.

GPS

Knows roughly where you are on Earth — but wanders by a few metres.

Wheels

Count how far they turned. On sand they spin without going anywhere.

Balance

Feels every turn and bump, like your inner ear. Slowly drifts off.

Eyes

Two cameras judging distance. Flat empty sand confuses them.

So the computer listens to all four, decides who to trust, and draws one map. Four imperfect answers, one good one. That is most of engineering.

08 / When you cannot buy it

Then you make it.
Or you ask.

The circuit board that runs the whole rover did not exist, so we designed our own and called it Kacchim. Same with the board that feeds it power.

The antennas we could buy were not good enough either. So we wrote to an antenna company in Finland and asked. Strangers, 7,000 km away, with no reason to reply.

They replied. Because the question was a good one. Ask the question — the worst answer is silence.

Dhaka ↔ Finland · designing an antenna together

Twenty-three companies now back this team

09 / The honest slide

It breaks.
Constantly.

2023 · the circuit burned

In a camp in the desert, hours from any shop. The team rebuilt it by hand in four hours and drove the next morning.

2024 · the body snapped

Mid-mission, in front of the judges. Fixed in six hours with only what was in the toolbox.

Everything we know, we learned from something breaking. If your project fails, you are not bad at this — you are doing it.

10 / Our score, every year

Getting better is not a straight line

The line goes down as often as it goes up. Behind every dip is a part that failed on the day — and behind every climb is the same team, carrying on. Seventh in the world, out of 116 teams.

11 / Not only rovers

We build drones too

Ours flies ahead of the rover and looks at the ground it cannot see yet, then radios back what is over the hill.

Solve one problem properly — knowing where you are — and it works in the air as well as on the ground.

The Bangladesh Navy came to our lab to see it. Students did the explaining.

Our lab, Dhaka · explaining the drone to Navy officers

12 / The best part of the job

Then we hand over the controller

A school ground, and a rover on it
Her turn to drive
Yes — it holds a person

Most of us are on this team because someone once let us touch a robot. That is literally the whole reason we came to your school today.

13 / Start this week

You do not need a lab. You need five things.

01Open something broken

A dead torch, an old toy, a fan that stopped. Take it apart. You cannot break what is already broken.

02Learn one skill properly

Soldering. Or one programming language. One, all the way — not five, halfway.

03Make it move

A motor, a battery, a switch. The first time something you built moves on its own, you are finished — you are an engineer now.

04Find two friends

Nobody builds a rover alone. Our team is a hundred people who each know one part very well.

05Enter something

A school science fair, an olympiad, a robotics contest. A deadline teaches faster than any book.

ANDAsk the question

Ask your teacher. Ask us, today, after this. We answer every message we get from a student.
