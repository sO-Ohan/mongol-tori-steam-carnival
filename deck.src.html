01 / Who we are

A student team
that never stopped

In 2015 a handful of BRAC University students decided Bangladesh should build a Mars rover. It followed BRAC Onnesha, the country's first nanosatellite, and Chondrobot, its lunar excavator. Eleven years later the team is still entirely student-run — juniors inherit the lab, the mistakes and the deadlines from the seniors who leave.

06 / Structure & mobility

Built for the terrain we could actually test on

Rocker-bogie, four wheels

Keeps every wheel loaded on broken ground. The chassis-to-rocker joint runs on tapered roller bearings because those loads are radial and axial at the same time the moment the rover turns on a slope.

Steel where it matters

A steel skeleton carries load; an aluminium box carries the electronics. TPU grips printed in the lab go on stainless wheels — traction and vibration damping for the cost of filament.

Simulated, then abused

Every rocker link, bearing housing and chassis joint went through FEA at maximum anticipated load. Then we drove it through mud, dust and rain in Savar until something complained.

2.5h Continuous operation at 30 °C — the batteries gave up before the rover did
<1s Full power-down from the master emergency stop, verified with a multimeter
5min Failed. The electronics box leaked in the 15-minute rain test. It is on the fix list, in writing.

07 / The arm

Seven degrees of freedom, and one cheat

The whole arm sits on a linear rail. Instead of solving reach with a longer, heavier, wobblier arm, we slide the entire base sideways. Reach goes up, inertia does not.

  • Heavy jointsLinear actuators drive shoulder, elbow and macro-pitch — mass stays near the chassis
  • Base rotationWorm gear · cannot be back-driven, so it holds position under load with no power
  • WristBLDC pitch, DC roll — 3-DOF end effector for fine work
  • GripperTPU-printed jaws on a metal frame · high-torque gear motor

Servicing tasks are scored on flipping switches and inserting plugs. That is a millimetre problem solved from a kilometre away.

09 / The antennas

We stopped guessing
and called an RF engineer

A radio's advertised range was measured somewhere that is not Bangladesh. So we sat down with the engineers at CompleTech — the Finnish antenna house behind ComAnt — and worked the problem properly.

  • Step 01Simulate our actual test grounds — terrain, clutter, mast height
  • Step 02Tune the antennas to those simulations, not to a generic pattern
  • Step 03MIMO omni on the rover, MIMO sector at base, Miniflex for 433 MHz
  • Step 04Measure the real range in the field before shipping a crate

11 / Power & safety

Designed, laid out and soldered by undergraduates

The power board

A backplane with one hot-swappable daughterboard per rail — 3.3 V, 5 V, 12 V, 19.5 V, 24 V, 48 V. A rail dies, you pull the card and push in a spare. In the pits, at night, with a head torch.

  • SwitchingEPC GaN FETs · LTC7891 buck + LM5125-Q1 boost
  • Efficiency≥95% — less heat to move, more minutes of mission
  • ProtectioneFuse per rail · reverse-polarity MOSFET on every input
  • TelemetryINA226 / INA228 stream live volts & amps to the base station

Two ways to stop it

One kill switch for the motors, one for the entire rover — deliberately identical circuits, so a spare part fixes either. IRF3205 MOSFETs driven by a MIC4422, triggered by hardware buttons or wirelessly through Kacchim, our own control board.

12 / Science payload

Looking for life, with a load cell and a drill

01 · Acquire

Auger drill

Cuts to depth in loose analog soil with torque margin and limit switches, so a jammed bit stops the motor instead of burning it.

02 · Contain

Sealed sample boxes

Locking lids and retention foam. A sample that spills on the drive home is a sample you never collected.

03 · Measure

Onboard load cells

Mass of every sample, weighed on the rover, to ±1 g against reference masses.

04 · Assay

Liquid handling + pH

Gasket-sealed transfer, calibrated probe, plus a vision pipeline for on-site markers and task verification.

The base station sends intent; the rover computer runs the mission logic; Kacchim drives the actuators and streams load-cell and motor feedback back up the 433 MHz link. Same board, same protocol as the rest of the rover — one thing to learn, one thing to fix.

13 / Debrief

Four things we'd tell a team starting today

01

Redundancy scores points. Performance wins arguments.

Three radio bands, four FPV feeds, two kill switches, hot-swap power rails. Nearly every mission we scored, we scored because a backup existed.

02

Test where you expect to fail.

Our electronics box leaked five minutes into a rain test. Finding that in Dhaka cost us a weekend. Finding it in Utah would have cost us the season.

03

Industry will pick up the phone.

CompleTech tuned our antennas. SATEL, SBG, Cytron, Ansys, Altium and twenty more back this team. None of that started with money — it started with a student sending a technical question.

04

The report is part of the rover.

Half the teams that never reach Utah are eliminated on paper. Write the design document as carefully as you cut the aluminium.
